Understanding Ductal Carcinoma In Situ (DCIS)

What Does "Stage 0 / In Situ" Mean?

"In situ" means "in its original place." DCIS is a non-invasive proliferation of abnormal epithelial cells confined completely inside the breast ductal basement membrane. Because these cells have not breached the duct wall, they cannot reach blood vessels or lymphatic channels.

Diagnostic Challenges & Breast Density

DCIS is typically detected via microcalcifications on mammograms. However, in patients with dense breast tissue, overlapping tissue can obscure calcifications, making clear diagnostic imaging and risk stratification more challenging.

Indolent vs. Progressive Risk

DCIS accounts for roughly 25% of new breast cancer diagnoses. While many lesions remain indolent and never progress, some can develop into Invasive Ductal Carcinoma (IDC) or second breast events—making accurate biomarker and microenvironment research vital.

Navigating DCIS Treatment Pathways

Educational Notice: This tool provides supplementary scientific information to help patients prepare questions for their care team. It is not a substitute for individualized medical advice.

Breast-Conserving Surgery (BCS)

Also known as a lumpectomy, BCS removes the abnormal DCIS lesion along with a small margin of healthy surrounding tissue while preserving the rest of the breast.

Radiation Therapy (RT) & Risk Stratification

Radiation therapy often follows BCS to lower local recurrence rates. Genomic and biosignature tools (such as DCISionRT) help clinicians distinguish low-risk from high-risk lesions to prevent both overtreatment and undertreatment.

Questions to Ask Your Oncology Team

  • Is my DCIS hormone-receptor positive (ER/PR)?
  • What is my estimated risk of local recurrence with or without radiation?
  • Are genomic or risk-stratification tests appropriate for my pathology?

Spatial Transcriptomics & Health Equity for Women of Color

Closing the Outcome Gap

Non-Hispanic Black women experience disproportionately higher rates of second breast events and breast cancer mortality following a DCIS diagnosis, yet remain underrepresented in precision oncology datasets and AI training cohorts.

Insights from Spatial Transcriptomics

Unlike bulk sequencing, spatial transcriptomics (such as 10x Genomics Visium) preserves tissue architecture—allowing researchers to map how myoepithelial cells and interferon signaling (IFN-α and IFN-γ) interact inside the DCIS tumor microenvironment.
